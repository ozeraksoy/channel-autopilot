# Script craft for the seven beats

| Beat | Job | Works | Fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| s1 hook (2–4 s) | Stop the scroll | A strange claim, an unfinished sentence, a question the viewer wants answered: "In Germany, when you don't understand a single thing, you say…" | Greetings, channel intro, "today we will…" |
| s2 reveal (1–3 s) | The payoff of the hook, big on screen | The phrase/fact itself, short; in the original language with a native voice when it is a foreign phrase | A long sentence nobody can read at a glance |
| s3 unpack (3–5 s) | Make sure everyone got it | The literal meaning / the plain statement, with a simple picture | Repeating s2 word for word |
| s4 story (12–20 s) | The joke | The claim acted out literally by the character, escalating in 3 steps, ending on the funniest image | Explaining instead of showing; more than one idea |
| s5 takeaway (3–5 s) | What it really means / why it matters | One line, plus an equivalent the audience already knows | A lecture |
| s6 example (4–6 s) | Make it usable | A two- or three-message chat where the phrase/fact lands as a punchline | An example that needs explaining |
| s7 CTA (4–6 s) | Comments and follows | A specific question ("what does YOUR language say when…?") + follow with a reason ("…for a new one every day") | "Like and subscribe" |

Checks before recording:
- Read the hook alone. Would you keep watching?
- Could a viewer with the sound off follow s4 from the pictures and captions?
- Is there exactly one joke, and is its funniest moment the cover frame?
- Does s7 contain the follow word and the comments word from the theme?
- Spoken length: count words; trim until it fits the target.

Vary on purpose across a batch (hook type, topic category, length) and record what you varied in `episodes.csv` →
`hook_type` / `experiment`. That is what makes the daily analysis able to say *why* something worked.
